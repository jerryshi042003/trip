## Key points
- [0:00:00] He joined TOKYO KNIT because his brand had few cut-and-sew and knit pieces, and he wanted to add more of them at that moment.
- [0:01:00] He was moved that these companies have kept the same methods going in Tokyo for generations, through 10 to 20 hard years; quality was stable, turnaround fast, and they proposed realistic ways to make difficult designs.
- [0:03:00] The "Y-neck" sweater: crew neck on the right half, V-neck on the left, pinched into a "Y" for Yuki. Uchida (the dyer) and he found they liked the same artist, which made agreeing on the striped pattern easy.
- [0:06:30] A jacquard started as a doodle on his iPad; the factory built the data from that small image. His houndstooth points the opposite way so it reads as a "Y." It was his first textile design and much simpler than he feared.
- [0:08:00] Interviewer's takeaway: if you and the factory share the finished image, you can make good things without technical jargon.
- [0:09:00] Maruwa (as heard) showed him 3D renders instead of a physical toile; he barely needed a toile at all. He says only the final product matters, and skipping a step was a fun discovery.
- [0:12:30] "Continuing is far harder than doing one big thing." His own family ran a clothing shop, so the multi-generation factories moved him.
