## Key points
- [0:03:00] TENDER PERSON is a duo of Bunka Fashion College classmates who began showing twice a year in 2014; at TOKYO KNIT 2023 they made the "Recycloop" collection from reclaimed cutting scraps, and YUKI HASHIMOTO joined as the JFW collaboration designer.
- [0:12:30] Hashimoto grew up on an island in Shiga, studied at Kyoto University of Art and Design, then spent about seven years in Belgium including a master's, and has run his brand in Japan since 2018.
- [0:14:00] Hashimoto says the Tokyo factories understood "what you are aiming for" from just a few words, at a level close to designers themselves, and that showing who made a garment, like produce in a supermarket, now matters.
- [0:17:30] Uchida of the Uchida dye works says Hashimoto's first design left them unsure it could be done; three or four phone calls plus email turned the image into a sample, and that trust is what leads to the next job.
- [0:34:00] Hashimoto's dresses start from contemporary and abstract art; prints felt flat, so he tried garment dyeing (bleaching red into all-black pieces) for the first time to get depth.
- [0:41:00] Hashimoto's main lesson: every Tokyo factory has its own character (the honor student, the one that gets a vague brief, the one that says "let's just try it"), and that mix of technique plus character is what can compete internationally.
- [0:46:30] TENDER PERSON's Yuto Yashige says he visits factories every season, takes prints straight to the print shop himself, and that some craftsmen see him as a young upstart, which can work in his favor.
